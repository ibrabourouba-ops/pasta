Our story

Built around a colour the kitchen wouldn't give up on.

Nero opened in 2018 with a stubborn idea: one ingredient, done with total commitment, beats a long menu done halfway.

It started with a single tasting menu course — a squid ink tagliolini served at a friend's pop-up — that the chef, Amine Belkacem, couldn't stop thinking about.

Within a year, the rest of the menu had quietly fallen away. What remained was pasta, made fresh every morning, coloured the same deep black, but built in a dozen different directions — crab, brown butter, ragù, bottarga.

The dining room followed the same instinct: take things out rather than add them. No music loud enough to compete with conversation, no menu longer than a single page, no plate that needs explaining before you eat it.

Today Nero seats 28 people a night across two services. The pasta is still rolled by hand, in the same corner of the kitchen, by whoever gets in first.
